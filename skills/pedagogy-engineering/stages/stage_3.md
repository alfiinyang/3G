# Stage 3: Learning Goal Formulation

Convert the broad educational intent and diagnosed problem into an overarching, learner-centered learning goal.

---

## 1. Principles of Goal Formulation

1. **Describe Learner Capability**: The goal must define the capability the learner will possess, not what the teacher or game will present.
2. **Never Merely Name Content**:
   - *Weak*: "Teach conditional statements."
   - *Strong*: "Develop learners' ability to use conditional logic to make decisions in simple programs."
   - *Weak*: "Ohm's Law module."
   - *Strong*: "Enable learners to predict and regulate electrical circuit behavior using Ohm's Law principles."
3. **Core Quality Criteria**:
   - **Relevant**: Solves the diagnosed gap from Stage 2.
   - **Achievable**: Realistic given stated scope, audience, and time constraints.
   - **Learner-Centred**: Framed from the perspective of what the learner will be able to do.
   - **Specific**: Concrete enough to guide objective decomposition.
   - **Cohesive**: Focused on a unified central capability.

---

## 2. Primary vs. Secondary Goals

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The specification must establish **ONE primary learning goal**.
> 
> Secondary goals are permitted only when genuinely essential to the primary goal and must be explicitly identified as subordinate.

---

## 3. Required Output Format

```markdown
### 3. Learning Goal Formulation
- **Primary Learning Goal**: [Comprehensive, learner-centered capability statement]
- **Goal Rationale**: [Explanation of how this goal directly addresses the diagnosed problem from Stage 2]
- **Secondary Learning Goals** *(optional, only if strictly necessary)*:
  - [Secondary Goal 1]: [Rationale and dependency on Primary Goal]
```
