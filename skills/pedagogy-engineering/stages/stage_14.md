# Stage 14: Transfer Requirements

Determine whether, how, and in what context learners must generalize their acquired knowledge beyond the immediate training environment.

---

## 1. Transfer Modalities

If transfer is required, determine the expected degree and type:

* **Near Transfer**: Applying the skill to structurally identical problems with surface variations (e.g., calculating Ohm's law with different resistor values or different circuit layouts).
* **Far Transfer**: Adapting principles to fundamentally different domains or contexts (e.g., applying hydraulic fluid flow models to understand electrical current; applying programming loop logic to project workflow automation).
* **Altered Conditions**: Executing the skill under time pressure, noise, incomplete data, or competing constraints.
* **Explanation / Justification**: Articulating why a solution works to a novel audience.
* **Adaptation / Refactoring**: Modifying an existing, working solution to fit an unexpected specification change.

---

## 2. Explicit Declaration Rule

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - If transfer is **not required** (e.g. narrow procedural training on a single software tool), **state this explicitly** with justification.
> - If transfer is **required**, it must be integrated into the Learning Progression (Stage 8) and the Evidence of Learning model (Stage 13).

---

## 3. Required Output Format

```markdown
### 14. Transfer Requirements
- **Transfer Status**: [Required / Not Required]
- **Target Transfer Type**: [Near Transfer / Far Transfer / Contextual Adaptation]
- **Transfer Scenarios / Contexts**:
  - [Context 1]: [Description of unfamiliar or real-world environment]
- **Transfer Tasks**:
  - [Task 1]: [Specific application task challenging learner generalization]
- **Evidence of Transfer**: [Observable performance proving deep understanding rather than superficial pattern matching]
```
